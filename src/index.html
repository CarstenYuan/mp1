Five Parks, One Endless Highway

Chasing red rock canyons and desert sunsets across the American West.

Arizona

Antelope Canyon

Antelope Canyon is a famous slot canyon located on Navajo land near Page, Arizona, known for its wavy sandstone walls and dramatic beams of light.

Utah

Bryce Canyon

Bryce Canyon National Park is a stunning natural reserve located in southwestern Utah, famous for having the world's largest collection of hoodoos—tall, thin spires of colorful rock.

California

Death Valley

Death Valley is a British comedy-crime drama series about an eccentric retired actor and a young Welsh detective who team up to solve murder mysteries in rural Wales.

Arizona

Grand Canyon

Grand Canyon is a massive, steep-sided gorge in Arizona carved by the Colorado River.

Utah

Zion National Park

Zion National Park is a prominent American national park in southwestern Utah known for its dramatic red-rock canyons, towering sandstone monoliths, and diverse ecosystems.

Before you go

Three Things Worth Knowing

Best Season

Spring and fall bring the mildest temperature. Desert parks can top 100F in summer and mountain roads may close in winter.

Trip Length

Plan 1-2 days per park for hiking and photography. A full loop through all five comfortably takes 7-10 days.

Driving Tips

Expect winding canyon roads and big day-night temperature swings. Rent with extra coverage, download offline maps, and check park entry reservations ahead of time.
